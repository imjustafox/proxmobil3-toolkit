# Keeping a cVEND reader awake and scanning

The FEIG cVEND reader inside your INIT PROXmobil3 locks into a 3-second heartbeat because that is almost certainly FEIG's **documented RF-field auto-timeout firing after idle**, compounded by a genuine **sub-1 mA hardware standby state that the datasheet says is left only "by digital input or timer"** — not by the status frames you are sending. FEIG's own ID ISC reader manuals describe an automatic "RF-Field minimum/maximum on-time" plus a "Power on-time" in configuration block CFG21, after which "the complete Reader will turn off automatically" ([FEIG ID ISC.PRH200 manual, p.22](https://manualshive.com/en/manual/obid/id-isc-prh200/3157489?page=22)); there is **no host "sleep" or "wake" opcode** anywhere in the documented FEIG command set, which means the fix is to never let the idle timer expire rather than to command a wake. The actionable answer has two halves that must be kept distinct: a *keepalive* that re-arms the reader faster than its sleep window, and a *graded reset ladder* that escalates from a benign probe up to a power cycle. The crucial honesty caveat is that every documented timer, mode, and reset opcode above belongs to FEIG's **public OBID/ID ISC protocol (STX 0x02 framing)**, and the cVEND speaks a *separate, closed* 0xBC "IPP" secure protocol — so the OBID mechanisms tell you what FEIG hardware does, while the exact IPP keepalive cadence, the working `0x10` reset sub-code, and whether CFG21-style timers are even host-reachable on the cVEND remain things we have had to reverse-engineer or still cannot confirm. Below, the power/sleep behavior comes first and in depth; the hardware, SDK/protocol, and transit-stack findings follow; and a closing table maps precisely where public FEIG documentation confirms our reverse-engineering, where our work goes beyond anything published, and where neither side has an answer.

## The sleep lock is an idle RF/power timeout, and the cure is a fast keepalive plus a reset ladder

### Why the reader goes quiet: documented FEIG behavior, not a mystery
The symptom — a reader that drops to a bare periodic heartbeat, stops scanning, and ignores status frames until power-cycled — matches two documented FEIG mechanisms stacked on top of each other. First, FEIG ID ISC readers **auto-switch the antenna RF field off after a configured idle on-time**: the "RF-Field minimum on-time" and "maximum on-time" parameters in **CFG21** cut antenna power once no transponder has been seen, and after a further "Power on-time" expires "*the complete Reader will turn off automatically*" ([FEIG ID ISC.PRH200 manual p.22](https://manualshive.com/en/manual/obid/id-isc-prh200/3157489?page=22)). Second, there is no escape hatch in software: a scan of the ID ISC.LR1002 documented command list (0x52, 0x63, 0x64, 0x66, 0x69, 0x6A, 0x6E, 0x72, 0x74, 0x80–0x83…) contains **no sleep, standby, power-down, or wake opcode at all** ([FEIG ID ISC.LR1002 System Manual](https://manualslib.com/manual/1732092/Feig-Electronic-Obid-Id-Isc-Lr1002.html)). Entering "sleep" is therefore a timer firing, and "exiting" it is configuration- or stimulus-driven, not a command. The cVEND hardware then adds a true low-power layer the OBID readers lack: FEIG's cVEND datasheet rates the module at **<1 mA in standby** with **"wake-up by digital input or timer"** ([FEIG cVEND plug flex product page](https://www.feig.de/en/payment-terminals/cvend-plug-flex/)). That single datasheet line is the best public explanation for why your status frames do not revive it — if the reader has dropped into the hardware standby state, the *intended* exits are a hardware digital-input assertion or a timer, and an in-band serial status frame may never reach the sleeping RF/CPU path at all. This also reframes the "3-second heartbeat": in FEIG's **Notification operating mode** the reader "pushes" buffered data and emits **keepalive messages that are never acknowledged** ([FEIG ID ISC.LR1002](https://manualslib.com/manual/1732092/Feig-Electronic-Obid-Id-Isc-Lr1002.html)), and on the cVEND IPP side the wiki documents a periodic **`0x07` Heartbeat** frame ([wiki.pm3.dev/cvend](https://wiki.pm3.dev/cvend)) — so the heartbeat you see is a liveness ping from a reader whose *scanning* has been timed off, not a sign the reader is healthy.

### Prevention: a keepalive that re-arms faster than the idle window
The robust strategy, transferred from FEIG's documented mode model and adapted to the IPP protocol we reverse-engineered, is to keep the reader continuously stimulated so the CFG21 idle timer never elapses. On documented OBID hardware this means driving the reader in **Host Mode** and issuing a lightweight command — Inventory `[0x01]`, Get Reader Info `[0x66]`, or Reader Diagnostic `[0x6E]` — on a cadence shorter than the RF-field minimum on-time, and treating a missed response as "wedged" ([FEIG ID ISC.LR1002](https://manualslib.com/manual/1732092/Feig-Electronic-Obid-Id-Isc-Lr1002.html)). On the cVEND's closed IPP link the analog, which our prior work and the community wiki together establish, is to send **`0x04` Status frames on a fixed cadence** — the wiki explicitly suggests `0x04` Status "to keep the reader awake" ([wiki.pm3.dev/cvend](https://wiki.pm3.dev/cvend)) — and, because status alone has proven insufficient in practice, to **periodically re-issue the arm sequence itself**: the `0xE4` per-type enables followed by the `0xAA 6afec201` commit that the reader acknowledges with `0xAB 0000`. The honest flag here is sharp: the `0x04`-keeps-it-awake claim is a *wiki suggestion that has not held up* (the same community record concedes the reader "eventually goes to sleep without any stimuli"), and re-arming a *running* reader with `0xAA`/`0xE4` **is not reproducibly reliable** — our own reverse-engineering could cold-start the reader deterministically but could not deterministically re-arm one that had already slept. A genuinely documented lever does exist but we cannot confirm it is reachable over IPP: if the cVEND exposes CFG21's RF-field on-time timers through its file/config operations (the wiki lists `0x96`–`0xA5` PutFile/GetFile/FileInfo file ops), lengthening or disabling the "RF-Field maximum on-time" and "Power on-time" would remove the trigger entirely — this is **documented FEIG behavior for OBID, pure inference for the cVEND**, and worth probing via the file-op interface.

### Recovery: a graded ladder from benign probe to power cycle
When the reader is already wedged, escalate in order of disruption and stop at the first rung that recovers scanning. The documented OBID reset ladder and our reverse-engineered cVEND equivalents line up rung-for-rung, but diverge in reliability — the cVEND's soft resets are the weak link, and a power cycle remains the only deterministic recovery we have.

| Rung | Documented OBID / ID ISC action | cVEND IPP equivalent (our RE) | Confidence |
|------|------------------------------|-------------------------------|------------|
| 1. Probe, don't assume dead | `[0x66]` Get Reader Info / `[0x6E]` Reader Diagnostic (reports temperature, control, impedance, power flags) ([LR1002](https://manualslib.com/manual/1732092/Feig-Electronic-Obid-Id-Isc-Lr1002.html)) | `0x02` Version / `0x04` Status — if a reply or `0x07` heartbeat arrives, the UART is alive and the fault is RF/mode, not the serial link | High (both documented) |
| 2. Re-arm RF without rebooting | `[0x6A]` RF Output ON (effect **depends on the active mode**), then `[0x69]` RF Reset (RF section only, field returns when reset completes) ([LR1002](https://manualslib.com/manual/1732092/Feig-Electronic-Obid-Id-Isc-Lr1002.html)) | Re-send `0xE4` enables + `0xAA 6afec201` arm | Low on cVEND — re-arm of a slept reader is **not reproducible** |
| 3. Soft reset, keep serial link | `[0x63]` RF-Controller Reset — RF field off after CPU reset, **communication interface NOT reset** ([LR1002](https://manualslib.com/manual/1732092/Feig-Electronic-Obid-Id-Isc-Lr1002.html)) | `0x10` Reset, sub-code `2` = restart app / `3` = reboot (first byte selects the action) ([wiki.pm3.dev/cvend](https://wiki.pm3.dev/cvend)) | Low — our RE found `0x10` **took the reader offline** rather than cleanly rebooting to a new `0x0F`; the clean sub-code is unidentified |
| 4. Full reboot, drops serial | `[0x64]` System Reset MODE 0x00 — resets main controller, **RF field off AND comm interface reset** ([LR1002](https://manualslib.com/manual/1732092/Feig-Electronic-Obid-Id-Isc-Lr1002.html)) | `0x10` sub-code `3` reboot / `4` poweroff | Low (same caveat as rung 3) |
| 5. Hardware power cycle | (external) | Cut and restore power (CL30 continuous, then momentary CL15), then **wait for the `0x0F` Startup frame** (up to ~60 s) before re-running the enable-and-arm handshake | **High — the only deterministic recovery today** |

Two documented gotchas sit alongside this ladder. **Baud Rate Detection `[0x52]` works only with the reader's real COM-ADR, never the broadcast `0xFF`** ([FEIG ID ISC.LR1002](https://manualslib.com/manual/1732092/Feig-Electronic-Obid-Id-Isc-Lr1002.html)) — a wrong-address probe makes a perfectly healthy reader look unresponsive, a trap worth ruling out before escalating. And FEIG's documented timing is built around **frame timeouts (a ~20 ms transparent-command no-transponder timeout, plus TR-RESPONSE-TIME), not hangs** ([FEIG ID ISC.LR1002](https://manualslib.com/manual/1732092/Feig-Electronic-Obid-Id-Isc-Lr1002.html)), which suggests a malformed IPP frame should time out rather than wedge the link — but that is unconfirmed for the cVEND and should be tested, not assumed. The practical conclusion from our reverse-engineering stands: **today a standalone reader should assume power-cycle-to-arm semantics** and gate the handshake on the `0x0F` Startup frame, because enables sent before that frame are silently dropped ([wiki.pm3.dev/cvend](https://wiki.pm3.dev/cvend)), and no software reset we have tried reliably regenerates that frame.

### The contactless duty-cycle angle: LPCD sensitivity can masquerade as sleep
One further mechanism can produce a "won't see the card" symptom that looks like sleep but is not. EMVCo-style contactless front ends increasingly use **Low Power Card Detection (LPCD)**: instead of continuous full-power polling, the reader emits "very short LPCD pulses," and only a field perturbation from an approaching card triggers a full poll ([Renesas PTX205R LPCD application note](https://www.renesas.com/en/document/apn/ptx205r-low-power-card-detection-lpcd-application-note?r=25612866)). Vendor guidance recommends "a programmable delay between sequential polling attempts," using a representative **~400 ms inter-poll delay** as a compromise, while warning that overly aggressive empty-field thresholds **reduce detection sensitivity** and that EMV contactless spec changes have *increased* the required power ([Analog Devices: Low Power Polling with Secure NFC Microcontrollers](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/design-notes/low-power-polling-with-maxim-secure-nfc-microcontrollers.html)). If the cVEND is in an LPCD-style idle rather than a true standby, a card that is too far or too fast can go unseen, making the reader appear asleep when it is actually polling at low sensitivity — an entirely different failure from the CFG21 power-down, and one that lengthening the active poll window or raising sensitivity would address. This is directional context from vendor app notes, not the EMVCo specification itself, and is **not confirmed for the cVEND's front end**.

## The hardware is a first-generation cVEND plug flex in its "DK" developer configuration

The reverse-engineered unit is, with high confidence, a **first-generation FEIG cVEND plug in the flex/DK (open-application-development) configuration**: the `/etc/issue` "cVEND Development Kit", the device self-identifying as a "cVEND Application", and the flex/"DK" (Development Kit) software configuration all converge on it, with INIT as the integrator who wrote the application layer on FEIG's secure Linux ([FEIG cVEND plug flex product page](https://www.feig.de/en/payment-terminals/cvend-plug-flex/)). The cVEND plug is FEIG's flush-mount module **purpose-built for transit validators and fare gates**, launched October 2015, first deployed as ~1,100 terminals on a Portland–Vancouver bus line — the exact reference architecture your PROXmobil3 instantiates ([FEIG "10 years of cVEND"](https://www.feig.de/en/news-en/10-years-of-cvend/)). **Buildroot 2014.05 places the unit firmly in Generation I**, not the later PCI PTS 6.x "plug II": Gen I shipped 128 MB RAM / 256 MB flash versus Gen II's 512 MB / 1 GB, so the RAM/flash size is a quick generation check ([FEIG cVEND plug flex product page](https://www.feig.de/en/payment-terminals/cvend-plug-flex/)). Electrically it is a ~79×70×31 mm, 5.0–5.5 V DC module on a **secure ARM9/Cortex-A-class CPU** with memory encryption, crypto acceleration, a TRNG, and tamper protection, carrying **four ID-000 SAM sockets** (via the SAM extension board), six LEDs, a buzzer, and — critically for your serial console — both a V.24 RS232 port and an **RS232-LVTTL low-voltage UART**, the latter being the likely home of the debug/IPP link ([FEIG cVEND plug flex product page](https://www.feig.de/en/payment-terminals/cvend-plug-flex/)). The contactless interface covers **ISO/IEC 14443 Type A/B, ISO/IEC 15693, NFC card emulation, and MIFARE**, with FCC filings confirming 13.56 MHz operation on an integrated antenna ([FCC ID PJMCVEND](https://fcc.report/FCC-ID/PJMCVEND/2598130.pdf)). Two things remain genuinely undocumented: the **exact RF transceiver IC** (an NXP CLRC663-class multiprotocol reader is a fit for the ISO14443A/B + 15693 + FeliCa + NFC envelope, but this is inference, not teardown-confirmed — FCC internal-photo PDFs returned HTTP 403), and the **meaning of the "cS01.05.45-49.00-2-2" model string**, which no public FEIG document decodes; "cS" plausibly abbreviates cVEND System/Software and "02.35" is the separately-reported application/firmware version, but the field structure is unverified pattern-matching.

## The IPP/BC protocol is a closed FEIG payment skin over a public OBID RF engine

FEIG runs two parallel protocol worlds, and the split is the key to reading your captures. The **public OBID/ID ISC "advanced protocol"** frames every command as `STX(0x02) | length(2, big-endian) | COM-ADR | control-byte | [mode] | data | CRC16`, scaling to 65535-byte frames, with a well-known command vocabulary ([FEIG CPR70 manual copy, Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/721525509/FEIG-CPR70-Manual); corroborated by [OBID classic-pro manual](https://manualslib.com/manual/1633605/Feig-Electronic-Obid-Classic-Pro-Series.html)). The cVEND's **0xBC "IPP" framing is categorically different** — it starts with `0xBC`, not `0x02`, and no public FEIG source documents a 0xBC magic byte anywhere ([GitHub amarok79/InlayTester.Drivers.FeigReader](https://github.com/amarok79/InlayTester.Drivers.FeigReader)) — confirming our conclusion that IPP is a *separate proprietary payment-host protocol* that merely shares the OBID protocol's *shape* (start byte, length prefix, command/address, CRC trailer). The two coexist by layering: the RF front-end is driven internally through the NDA **`feclr` ("FEIG ContactLess Reader") library** — the cVEND counterpart to the public `feisc`, consumed by the firmware's `RFIDReaderMultiApp.cpp` — while the external link to the PROXmobil3 uses IPP; exhaustive code search finds **zero public references to any `feclr_*` symbol**, so this library is genuinely closed ([the SDK is distributed only via `cvend-support@feig.de`](https://manualmachine.com/feigelectronic/cvend/11347140-users-manual)). Where public documentation *does* confirm our reverse-engineering is the card-command layer carried inside IPP payloads. The **DESFire native opcodes** we see — `0x5A` SelectApplication, `0x0A`/`0x1A`/`0xAA` Authenticate, `0x60` GetVersion, `0xBD`/`0x3D` Read/Write, and the all-important `0xAF` additional-frame continuation — match the public DESFire command set exactly ([python-desfire supported commands](https://python-desfire.readthedocs.io/en/latest/supported-commands/); [NXP AN12752](https://www.nxp.jp/docs/en/application-note/AN12752.pdf)), and the firmware's reference to `m075031_desfire.pdf` is the characteristic NXP (NDA) document-number format for the DESFire functional spec the developers worked from. The **Ultralight-C opcodes** are likewise public and match the `ProxCardUltralightC` handler: READ `0x30`, WRITE `0xA2`, and the `0x1A`/`0xAF` two-part 3DES AUTHENTICATE handshake ([NXP MF0ICU2 datasheet](https://www.nxp.com/docs/en/data-sheet/MF0ICU2.pdf); [nfc-tools Ultralight C reference](https://nfc-tools.github.io/resources/rfid-tags/mifare-ultralight-c)). And `emvco_ep_configure` / `emvco_ep_preprocess` are verbatim **EMVCo "Entry Point" (Book B)** function names — the terminal-side polling and pre-processing stage — confirming the firmware embeds a real EMVCo L1 front-end plus L2 kernels, consistent with the cVEND's EMVCo/PCI certification.

## The transit stack is a multi-application handler table where DESFire carries every closed-loop scheme

The firmware's enabled "ProxCard" handlers — **VdvKa(4), MifareClassic(5), Iso(6), Desfire(7), Girogo(8), Itso(9), UltralightC(10)** — are the concrete realization of FEIG's marketed "**eight independent application directories**" that process open-loop payment "in parallel with closed-loop ticketing… with the same priority" ([FEIG press: multi-application technique](https://www.feig.de/en/news/press-news/detail/view/feigs-multi-application-technique-gives-a-boost-to-transaction-speed-of-mass-transit-systems/); [FEIG cVEND plug flex datasheet](https://feig.de/wp-content/uploads/Data_sheet_cVEND_plug_flex_EN.pdf)). The index numbers look like entries in a fixed handler-type enum, with slots 0–3 (none/raw/EMV/FeliCa) not enabled on this unit, and "Iso(6)" most plausibly a generic ISO 14443-4 / 7816 APDU base used by the higher schemes. The decisive insight for understanding the handlers is that **DESFire is the physical carrier for the logical transit applications**, so several handlers overlap at the chip level: ITSO's "Shell" is mapped onto DESFire files in the current Part 10 revision (which added "placeholders for DESFire and Calypso" and marked MIFARE Classic 1K obsolete) ([ITSO technical specification](https://www.itso.org.uk/itso-specification/itso-technical-specification)), and Germany's VDV-KA (((eTicket stores its entitlement — the **Berechtigung/EFS in TLV format** with an application logbook and MAC-protected control records — on microprocessor cards that are now commonly DESFire ([VDV control-verification doc, 2017](https://media.frag-den-staat.de/files/foi/565526/20171122KontrollnachweiseEFS_geschwaerzt.pdf); [Intelligent Transport](https://www.intelligenttransport.com/transport-articles/10003/eticket-germany/)). This is why `Itso(9)`, `VdvKa(4)`, and `Desfire(7)` are not redundant: DESFire is the platform, the scheme handlers are the logical applications layered on it, and each needs its scheme keys held in one of the four SAMs. `Girogo(8)` is a legacy artifact — the German contactless prepaid purse was **wound down 2020–2024/2025** ([Sparkasse GeldKarte FAQ](https://www.sparkasse.de/unsere-loesungen/privatkunden/karten/geldkarte.html); [Wikipedia: GeldKarte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GeldKarte)), so that handler is almost certainly dormant in any current deployment. The EMV side explains a behavior that mirrors the sleep problem in spirit: the **Entry Point cannot poll until it is configured**. Each supported brand must be provisioned as a "reader combination" (AID + Kernel ID + transaction limits) per Book A Tables 5-2/5-3; with no valid combination set, the Entry Point has nothing to match a card against and will not start ([US Patent 11138583](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11138583); [US Patent 11107055](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11107055)). For open-loop transit the amount at tap is typically zero (fare computed later by the back office in an account-based-ticketing flow), so `emvco_ep_preprocess` is configured for a zero-amount/deferred-authorization path rather than a retail sale — the validator captures a token and the cloud does the fare math ([Masabi: account-based ticketing](https://blog.masabi.com/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-about-account-based-ticketing)). Across all of this, **raw UID capture needs none of it**: ISO/IEC 14443-3 collects the UID during anticollision/SELECT *before* any authentication, which is why our standalone recovery of UID `04:50:67:0A:81:22:90` from a `0xBE` UnhandledCard frame required no SAM, no fare keys, and no application context at all.

## Conclusion

The most useful shift from this research is diagnostic: the sleep lock is not a bug to be out-clevered with a magic wake frame but the **expected behavior of a FEIG RF idle timer and a sub-1 mA hardware standby**, and the only wake paths FEIG documents are a shorter-than-idle keepalive, a configuration change to the RF on-time timers, a hardware digital-input/timer assertion, or a power cycle — in that order of preference, with the power cycle currently the only deterministic one on the cVEND because its soft-reset sub-codes behave unreliably and its re-arm-while-running path is not reproducible. The honest boundary is that *every* documented timer and reset opcode belongs to FEIG's public OBID protocol, so they describe what the silicon does but not the exact IPP bytes to send; the highest-leverage next experiments are therefore to probe whether the cVEND's file-op interface (`0x96`–`0xA5`) exposes CFG21-style RF on-time parameters we could lengthen, and to sweep the `0x10` Reset sub-codes for one that regenerates the `0x0F` Startup frame without a power cut.

The second takeaway is how cleanly the public record now brackets our reverse-engineering. Everything *inside* the air interface and the payment standards — Ultralight-C and DESFire opcodes, the EMVCo Entry-Point gating, the multi-application handler model — is fully corroborated by NXP, EMVCo, and ITSO documentation, which means our interpretation of the IPP *payloads* rests on solid ground. Everything *about the IPP envelope itself* — the 0xBC framing, the `0xAA` arm command, the JAMCRC trailer, the file-op protocol, the cold-boot scan recipe — is ours alone, filling a gap that FEIG deliberately keeps closed behind `cvend-support@feig.de` to protect the payment kernel, not the UID path. And a short, well-defined list of unknowns (the `0x10` clean-reboot sub-code, the "cS…" string, the `0x9C` FileInfo format, the secure SAM/transparent opcodes) is now explicitly bounded on both sides — the right targets for either the next sniff or a formal NDA request to FEIG.
