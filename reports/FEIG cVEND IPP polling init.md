# Arming a cVEND reader to emit UIDs

> **Update (2026-11-21) — superseded framing.** Later live-hardware work changed
> the headline of this report. The cVEND scans ISO14443 **autonomously** the moment
> its own firmware finishes booting: it needs no `0xE4` enable, no `0xAA`, and no
> cert read to emit UIDs — raw cards arrive as `0xBE` UnhandledCard frames. Sending
> the init sequence below actually *stops* the autonomous scan and switches the
> reader into nx's handled-event mode (`0xD5` per enabled type), which is a feature,
> not a prerequisite for UIDs. `0xAA 6afec201` is **SetTime** (confirmed by the
> firmware handler `IppHandlerSetTime`), not an "arm" command; it is incidental to
> scanning. The soft-reset "open problem" below is also solved: `0x10` **subcode
> `02`** (restart application) cleanly reboots the reader to a fresh `0x0F` Startup
> in ~6 s with the serial link preserved; a *bare* `0x10` is what wedges it. Treat
> the enable/arm handshake below as a faithful record of what nx does, not as the
> way to start scanning. The authoritative current account is **INTERFACE.md**
> (§NFC, §Sleep) and **PROCESS.md**.

A stock-firmware serial sniff has finally produced the missing recipe: on the INIT PROXmobil3, you drive its onboard FEIG cVEND reader to raw ISO14443 UID events over the 0xBC "IPP" serial link with a four-step host-to-reader sequence — a `0x02` version read, one `0xE4` "enable card function" per card type, a previously-undocumented `0xAA 6afec201` commit/arm command that the reader acknowledges with `0xAB 0000`, and a `0xE8 04fe0a0000` read that returns the reader's X.509 device certificate. The CONNECTED→INITIALIZED transition the stock `nx` firmware logs is not a cryptographic challenge-response: it is this plain enable-then-arm handshake followed by a host-side *read* of the device certificate's `notAfter` date for display/validation. The single decisive constraint is timing, not keys — the reader only begins polling after it finishes its own power-on boot, which it signals with a `0x0F` "Startup" frame up to roughly a minute after power-up; enables sent before that frame are silently dropped. Once armed, the reader auto-scans and reports every tag it sees: `0xD5` card-in/out frames carrying a 7-byte UID for enabled card types, and `0xBE` "UnhandledCard" frames carrying the UID for types left disabled. This was proven end-to-end with no `nx` running: a real card UID `04:50:67:0A:81:22:90` was recovered from a `0xBE` frame using only this sequence, confirming that raw UID capture needs no application, SAM, or fare keys whatsoever. The public record — `wiki.pm3.dev` plus FEIG's datasheets — documents the frame format and the `0xE4` enable step but never the `0xAA` arm command, the certificate read, or a cold-boot standalone recipe; those gaps are what the empirical work closes.

## The handshake is an enable-and-arm sequence, not a crypto challenge

Everything rides on one frame format, confirmed both by the community wiki and by the firmware sniff: `BC seq replyTo type lenHi lenLo crc8 [payload] [crc32]`. Byte 0 is always the magic `0xBC`; byte 1 is a sequence counter that increments from 1 and skips 0 on rollover; byte 2 is `replyTo` (the sequence number being answered, or `0x00` for unsolicited reader events); byte 3 is the message type; bytes 4–5 are the big-endian payload length; byte 6 is a CRC-8 (Dallas/Maxim) over the preceding six header bytes; the payload follows; and **only for message types ≥ 0x80 does a trailing 4-byte CRC follow the payload** ([wiki.pm3.dev/cvend](https://wiki.pm3.dev/cvend)). The link itself is `/dev/ttymxc3` (aliased `/dev/ttyS3`), 115200 8N1 ([wiki.pm3.dev/cvend](https://wiki.pm3.dev/cvend)).

The reverse-engineered host→reader sequence that takes a freshly booted reader from CONNECTED to INITIALIZED and starts it scanning, captured by LD_PRELOAD-sniffing the stock `nx` firmware's serial writes, is four steps:

1. **`0x02` Version request.** The host reads device ID, firmware, and working directory; the reader answers with `0x03` VersionReply. This matches the public message table ([wiki.pm3.dev/cvend](https://wiki.pm3.dev/cvend)).
2. **`0xE4` Enable card function — one frame per card type.** Payload is `00 <id> 01 01`, and the reader acknowledges each with a `0xE5` ProxCardFunctionReply carrying status `0000`. The function IDs are `4`=VdvKa, `5`=MifareClassic, `6`=ISO, `7`=DESFire, `8`=Girogo, `9`=ITSO, `10`=UltralightC. The wiki documents this exact opcode and ID table but shows the stock PM3 firmware enabling only DESFire (`00 07 01 01`) at startup ([wiki.pm3.dev/cvend](https://wiki.pm3.dev/cvend)); the sniff shows the real firmware enabling the full set it cares about, one `0xE4` per type.
3. **`0xAA` payload `6afec201` → reader replies `0xAB 0000`.** This is the missing piece. It is a **commit/arm command** that the reader answers with a success status, and it appears in no public source — not `wiki.pm3.dev`, not any FEIG datasheet. (Note a labeling trap: the community wiki's message table names `0xAA`/`0xAB` "SetTime"/"SetTimeReply" ([wiki.pm3.dev/cvend](https://wiki.pm3.dev/cvend)); empirically, with the `6afec201` payload, the exchange functions as the arm/commit that follows the enables. The in-folder empirical finding governs.)
4. **`0xE8` payload `04fe0a0000` → reader returns `0xE9`** carrying its X.509 device certificate. This is the host-side "device certificates valid until…" read that precedes `nx`'s CONNECTED→INITIALIZED transition. It is a *read for host validation*, not a challenge-response — the host fetches the cert and inspects its expiry, and the reader does not demand anything of the host in return. The wiki lists `0xE8` SecurityServices with cert subcommands (ImportX509Cert, GenerateKSKPair, etc.) but publishes no flow ([wiki.pm3.dev/cvend](https://wiki.pm3.dev/cvend)); the sniff supplies the actual payload and its role.

**Timing is the real gate.** The reader only begins polling after it completes its own power-on boot, which it announces with a `0x0F` "Startup" frame up to roughly a minute after power-up — the wiki notes the same frame arrives "approx 1 minute after power on" ([wiki.pm3.dev/cvend](https://wiki.pm3.dev/cvend)). Enables sent before the `0x0F` are silently dropped with no acknowledgement. So the correct host logic is: open the UART, wait for the unsolicited `0x0F`, then run steps 1–4. The wiki's own "sample reader flow" hints at the sleep problem without solving it, suggesting the host keep the reader awake with periodic `0x04` Status frames ([wiki.pm3.dev/cvend](https://wiki.pm3.dev/cvend)).

One correction to the public record is worth stating plainly: the wiki frames "the two 0xBC messages" (body `0x60` = DESFire GetVersion, and body `5A2BC3F9` = DESFire SelectApplication) as what the host sends "after initialization" ([wiki.pm3.dev/cvend](https://wiki.pm3.dev/cvend)). Those are **application-level DESFire reads against a present card**, not the init/enable handshake. They presuppose an already-armed reader and a tapped DESFire card, and they are irrelevant to obtaining a raw UID.

## cVEND inherits the public OBID RF engine but hides its payment skin

FEIG runs two parallel protocol worlds, and understanding the split explains why the enable-and-arm sequence looks the way it does. The **OBID / ID ISC** RFID readers speak a fully public "FEIG Reader Protocol" in which a single control byte names each command: `0x52` Baud Rate Detection, `0x63` CPU Reset, `0x65` Get Software Info, `0x69` RF Reset, `0x80`–`0x83` the configuration block, and `0x0B 0x01` (or the older per-standard `0xB0 0x01`) Inventory, which runs ISO anticollision and returns the transponder UID ([Amarok79/InlayTester.Drivers.FeigReader](https://github.com/Amarok79/InlayTester.Drivers.FeigReader); [FEIG i-scan ID ISC.PR101 user manual](https://manualmachine.com/feigelectronic/obidiscanidiscmr101/23026941-user-manual)). The public serial library is `feisc` (`FEISC_RF_RESET = 0x69`, `FEISC_ISO_COMMAND = 0xB0`), with FEDM as the C++ layer above it ([feisc.h, ROS rfid_drivers doxygen](https://docs.ros.org/en/hydro/api/rfid_drivers/html/feisc_8h.html)).

The **cVEND** payment terminals are a separate secure line. FEIG's own cVEND user manual says the reader "offers an easy to use secure protocol" and "can be used in payment mode as well as in transparent card reader mode," gating all support behind `cvend-support@feig.de` ([Feig cVEND User Manual, via manualmachine](https://manualmachine.com/feigelectronic/cvend/11347140-users-manual)). Every cVEND datasheet states "Development devices, tools and SDK available on request" ([cVEND compact II datasheet](https://www.feig.de/wp-content/uploads/FEIG_Data-sheet-cVEND-compact-II-Platform-for-App-Development.pdf); [cVEND plug flex datasheet](https://feig.de/wp-content/uploads/Data_sheet_cVEND_plug_flex_EN.pdf)). The INIT PROXmobil3 validator is built on exactly this hardware — an ITSO certificate lists "PROXmobil3 RSP Rail Validator, version 20.36 (Feig 04.19)" ([ITSO certificate c00202](https://itso.org.uk/certificate/10292.1/c00202-init-proxmobil3-rsp-rail-validator)).

This is why the IPP `0xBC` protocol does *not* look like the OBID `0x0B 0x01` inventory call. cVEND wraps the same RF front-end in a proprietary secure application protocol: instead of the host issuing an inventory command and parsing a tag table, the host *enables card types and arms the reader*, and the reader then *pushes* card events asynchronously (`0xD5`, `0xB1`, `0xBE`). The `feclr`-style "FEIG Contactless Reader" library that the PROXmobil3's `RFIDReaderMultiApp.cpp` component consumes is the cVEND counterpart to the public `feisc`, and it is NDA-only — exhaustive code search found **zero** public references to any `feclr_*` symbol or to `RFIDReaderMultiApp` ([GitHub code search, 2026-11-19, no public URL]). The secrecy is a payment-certification posture: cVEND terminals are PCI PTS certified, run a "cVEND specific secure Linux operating system," and are provisioned at FEIG's PCI P2PE-certified Key Injection Facility ([cVEND compact II datasheet](https://www.feig.de/wp-content/uploads/FEIG_Data-sheet-cVEND-compact-II-Platform-for-App-Development.pdf); [FEIG PayServ RKI](https://www.feig.de/en/payserv-solutionsuite-rki/)). The protocol is withheld to protect the payment kernel, not the UID path.

One protocol detail the community explicitly could not crack is now confirmed: the trailing 4-byte CRC on type-≥0x80 frames is **CRC-32/JAMCRC** — the standard CRC-32 bit-inverted, little-endian. The wiki author wrote that this CRC "doesn't match any polynomial they recognize" ([wiki.pm3.dev/cvend](https://wiki.pm3.dev/cvend)); the sniff identifies it, which means a builder can now generate valid `0xE4`/`0xAA`/`0xE8` frames (all < 0x80, so header-CRC-8 only) *and* validate or synthesize the ≥0x80 application frames.

## The certificate step reads an identity, it does not negotiate one

The `0xE8 04fe0a0000` → `0xE9` exchange returns the reader's actual X.509 device certificate: issued by the **FEIG Production Terminal CA**, **DEVICE ID 17EE5468**, valid **2020-05-04 to 2030-05-02**. This is the concrete object behind the `nx` log line "device certificates valid until <date>" that sits between CONNECTED and INITIALIZED. Critically, it is a **one-way read for host-side validation**, not a mutual-auth or TLS-like handshake and not a gate the host must satisfy with its own credential. The host asks the reader for its certificate, reads the `notAfter` field, and proceeds.

This resolves a question the web research could only hypothesize. FEIG's platform genuinely carries "manufacturer-issued X.509 device certificates used for key encryption and device authentication" ([SFEY TPS Device API, Key Management](https://sfeydocs.sfey.com/docs/doc-api-spec/API%20v3.0/TPS%20Device%20API/Terminal%20Registration/03%20Key%20Management%20Automatic)), and the cert-expiry-then-INITIALIZED boot pattern looked like it *could* be a cryptographic attestation. It is not — at least not on the serial link and not as a prerequisite the host must defeat to read UIDs. The expensive, PCI-driven cryptography (remote key injection, EMV secure messaging) lives elsewhere: the RKI channel is a separate "secure connection" to FEIG's infrastructure ([FEIG PayServ RKI](https://www.feig.de/en/payserv-solutionsuite-rki/)), and fare-data decryption needs the application SAM keys. The startup cert read is cheap and informational. A builder who wants only UIDs can treat step 4 as optional diagnostics — the arm command in step 3 is what actually starts the scan.

## No keys are needed: a real UID came out of a 0xBE frame standalone

The standards layer already predicts this, and the empirical result confirms it. In ISO/IEC 14443-3, the UID is collected during the REQA/anticollision/SELECT sequence *before* any authentication; MIFARE/DESFire/Calypso key login only happens after selection ([ISO/IEC 14443-3:2018 TOC](https://committee.iso.org/standard/73598.html)). So once a cVEND is armed, emitting a UID requires no application, fare, or SAM keys.

The proof: with **no `nx` running**, driving the reader with only the enable-and-arm sequence above, a real card's UID `04:50:67:0A:81:22:90` was read from a `0xBE` "UnhandledCard" frame. The `0xBE` frame is what the reader emits for a card whose type was *not* enabled — it still reports the UID (the wiki describes `0xBE` UnhandledCard as carrying "UID + historical bytes" for a "card type not enabled") ([wiki.pm3.dev/cvend](https://wiki.pm3.dev/cvend)). For *enabled* types, the reader instead pushes a `0xD5` frame: byte 0 is `0x00` (card entered) or `0x01` (card left), byte 1 is `0x00`, and bytes 2–8 are the 7-byte UID ([wiki.pm3.dev/cvend](https://wiki.pm3.dev/cvend)). Either path yields a raw UID with zero key material.

This is a genuine advance over the published community record. Every prior card-touching project worked *around* the init problem rather than solving it: `checkraisefold/rust-feig-app` is a passive parser that opens the port and prints frames but **never writes a byte** ([rust-feig-app main.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/checkraisefold/rust-feig-app/main/src/main.rs)), and `Jarynnnn/PROXMobil3-HOP` boots the stock `nx` for about 20 seconds purely to let it initialize the NFC hardware and create the serial links, then kills `nx` and passively sniffs taps ([PROXMobil3-HOP autorun.sh](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jarynnnn/PROXMobil3-HOP/main/autorun.sh)). As the community notes conceded, "no published, reproducible 'open the port cold, send these exact bytes, get a UID without NX' recipe exists" ([wiki.pm3.dev/cvend](https://wiki.pm3.dev/cvend)). It exists now.

**Minimal builder recipe for a standalone UID reader:** open `/dev/ttymxc3` at 115200 8N1 → wait for the unsolicited `0x0F` Startup frame (budget ~60 s after a cold power-up) → send `0x02` Version → for each wanted card type send `0xE4` `00 <id> 01 01` and wait for `0xE5` status `0000` → send `0xAA` `6afec201` and wait for `0xAB 0000` → optionally send `0xE8` `04fe0a0000` to read the device cert → then consume unsolicited `0xD5` (enabled types, 7-byte UID at offset 2) and `0xBE` (disabled types, UID in payload) events, keeping the reader awake with periodic `0x04` Status frames. Build every frame with the CRC-8 Dallas/Maxim header checksum; for any type-≥0x80 frame you receive or send, validate/append the CRC-32/JAMCRC trailer.

## Open problems and next steps

Two problems remain genuinely unsolved and will bite a builder. First, **re-arming an already-running reader is not reliable.** The clean path above assumes a *fresh cold boot* that produces the `0x0F` Startup frame; the reader sleeps when idle, and re-arming polling on a reader that is already up (rather than power-cycled) does not reproducibly work. The wiki observed the same sleep behavior and could only suggest periodic `0x04` Status keep-alives ([wiki.pm3.dev/cvend](https://wiki.pm3.dev/cvend)). Second, soft reset needs the right sub-code. The `0x10` Reset opcode's first byte selects the action (2 = restart app, 3 = reboot, 4 = poweroff, 33–36 = reset SAM 1–4) per the wiki ([wiki.pm3.dev/cvend](https://wiki.pm3.dev/cvend)). A *bare* `0x10` with no sub-code took the reader offline — which is the dead end originally hit here — but **`0x10 02` (restart application) was later found to reliably reboot the reader to a fresh `0x0F` Startup in ~6 s with the serial link preserved** (`./pm3.py cvend-restart`; see INTERFACE.md §Sleep). So both problems in this section are now resolved: idle-sleep is recoverable in software without a power cycle, and raw UIDs need no arming at all.

The practical consequence is that a robust standalone reader today should assume **power-cycle-to-arm** semantics: cut and restore power (the hardware boot path is CL30 continuous then momentary CL15, per the PROXmobil3 hardware notes ([kevin.wallace.seattle.wa.us/pm3](https://kevin.wallace.seattle.wa.us/pm3/))), wait for `0x0F`, then run the handshake once. The next investigations worth doing: sweep the `0x10` Reset sub-codes to find a software restart that reliably re-emits `0x0F`; characterize the exact idle-sleep timeout and whether `0x04` Status (or `0xAA` re-arm) wakes a slept-but-armed reader without a full re-enable; and transcribe the linked `cvend-bootup.sal` logic-analyzer capture ([wiki.pm3.dev/cvend](https://wiki.pm3.dev/cvend)) against the now-known frame grammar to see whether the stock firmware does anything between `0x0F` and `0x02` that the sniff did not surface.

The deeper takeaway reframes what "secure" meant here. The cVEND's forbidding posture — NDA SDK, PCI certification, X.509 device certs, a Key Injection Facility — guards the *payment* path, and the community reasonably assumed a cryptographic wall stood between a cold reader and any card data. It does not. The wall is a one-way certificate *read* and a boot-timing dependency, both trivially satisfiable from the host side, and the raw ISO14443 UID was always a pre-authentication artifact of the air protocol itself. The genuinely hard, undocumented piece turned out to be a single four-byte "arm" payload (`0xAA 6afec201`) and the discipline to wait for the `0x0F` Startup frame before sending it — not cryptography at all.
